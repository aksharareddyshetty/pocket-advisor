# F-1 work and status rules

Federal rules that apply at every US school. Schools add their own steps and deadlines on top, so always pair this
file with the student's school page (see `schools.md`). Checked on 2026-10-24 against the federal regulation and
ASU's international office pages. Rules change: read the live official page before giving a firm answer, and say
the date you read it.

This is general information, not legal advice. Anything that could cost the student their status ends with
"confirm with your DSO" and a drafted email.

Primary sources:
- 8 CFR 214.2(f), the F-1 regulation: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/214.2
- Study in the States (DHS): https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students
- SEVP Portal (OPT reporting): https://sevp.ice.gov/opt/
- USCIS Form I-765 (OPT applications): https://www.uscis.gov/i-765
- E-Verify employer search: https://www.e-verify.gov/
- Form I-983 (STEM training plan): https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/i983.pdf

## 1. Staying full-time

- Undergraduates need at least 12 credit hours per fall and spring term (8 CFR 214.2(f)(6)(i)(B)).
  Graduate minimums are set by the school, often 9.
- Online classes: at most one class or 3 credits per term count toward the full-time minimum
  (8 CFR 214.2(f)(6)(i)(G)). Schools can be stricter. ASU requires 9 in-person credits for undergraduates and
  6 for graduates.
- Dropping below full-time without an approved Reduced Course Load (RCL) from the DSO is a status problem.
  Tell the student to get RCL approval before dropping, not after. Swapping a class is safer than drop-then-add.
- Summer is usually a vacation term, unless it is the student's first or final term.
- Audited classes do not count.

## 2. Can I take this job?

Work through these in order and stop at the first answer.

1. Is the student on F-1? If J-1 or another status, say these steps are for F-1 and send them to their
   international office.
2. Has the student finished their program (degree conferred, or past the program end date on the I-20)?
   - Yes: the only route is post-completion OPT (section 5) or STEM OPT (section 6). They need the EAD card in hand
     and its start date reached.
3. Is the employer the school itself, or a business on campus that serves students (bookstore, dining, campus
   cafe)? That is on-campus work (section 3).
4. Anything else is off-campus. Before graduation that needs CPT (section 4) or pre-completion OPT, authorized
   before the first day of work.
5. Unpaid internships, volunteering for a company, remote work for any employer while in the US, freelance,
   gig apps and paid social posts: treat as needing authorization and send them to their DSO (section 9).

Answer with one of: "On-campus, OK within the hours limit", "Needs CPT", "Needs OPT", "Not allowed yet", or
"Ask your DSO first", plus the one-line reason and the official link.

## 3. On-campus work

- Up to 20 hours a week while school is in session; full-time during official breaks and summer vacation
  (8 CFR 214.2(f)(9)(i)). Schools often cap breaks at 40 hours a week.
- The limit is combined across all on-campus jobs, including stipends.
- ASU counts each Monday to Sunday week on its own (no averaging over a pay period) and caps summer at 20 hours if
  summer is the student's first or final term.
- No on-campus work after the program completion date. Working past it is unauthorized employment.
- New students may start up to 30 days before the I-20 program start date (ASU wording).

## 4. CPT (Curricular Practical Training)

- Off-campus work that is an integral part of the curriculum, usually an internship course.
- Usually requires one full academic year of full-time enrollment first (graduate programs that require it right
  away are an exception).
- The DSO authorizes CPT on a new I-20 for a specific employer and dates. Work may not begin before that.
- Part-time CPT is 20 hours a week or less; full-time is more.
- 12 months or more of full-time CPT makes the student ineligible for post-completion OPT. Part-time CPT does not.
- 1099 contractor arrangements do not qualify (ASU).
- Current news: in August 2026 SEVP issued guidance that CPT should be authorized only when practical training is
  required for everyone in the degree program. Schools are reviewing it. Check the school's CPT page before
  answering and quote what it says.

## 5. Post-completion OPT

- Up to 12 months of work directly related to the field of study, at least 20 hours a week to count as employed.
- Filing window: USCIS accepts Form I-765 from 90 days before to 60 days after the program end date, and within
  30 days of the DSO's OPT recommendation on the I-20. Many schools (ASU included) accept the request a few days
  earlier (97 days) so the I-20 is ready on day 90.
- The student picks a start date within 60 days after the program end date. It cannot be changed after filing.
- No work until the EAD is in hand and its start date has arrived.
- Unemployment limit: 90 days total during the 12 months. Unemployment days start counting on the EAD start date.
  Jobs under 20 hours a week count as unemployed.
- Report employer, job and address changes within 10 days (SEVP Portal for employer info at ASU).

## 6. STEM OPT extension

- 24 more months for degrees on the DHS STEM list.
- Employer must be enrolled in E-Verify and in good standing, have an EIN, and complete Form I-983 with the student.
  1099 arrangements do not qualify.
- Filing: USCIS accepts the application up to 90 days before the current OPT end date and within 60 days of the
  STEM I-20 issue date. It must arrive before the OPT end date.
- Unemployment limit rises to 150 days total across OPT and STEM OPT.
- Validation reports to the school every 6 months; self-evaluations at 12 and 24 months.

## 7. Travel signature

- To re-enter the US, the I-20 needs a DSO travel signature.
- Valid 12 months for enrolled students, 6 months for students on OPT, or until the I-20 (or EAD) expires,
  whichever comes first (ASU wording; most schools use the same periods).
- ASU accepts the request up to 120 days before the return date.

## 8. Reporting changes

- Address changes within 10 days.
- On OPT: employer name, address and start or end dates within 10 days.
- Changes in major, degree level or program end date go through the DSO first.

## 9. Never allowed without authorization

Say these plainly when they come up, then point to the DSO:

- Gig and delivery apps (DoorDash, Uber, Instacart) and freelancing.
- Being paid by a brand for social media posts.
- Remote work for an employer while physically in the US, including a company back home.
- Starting an off-campus job, paid or unpaid, before CPT or the EAD start date.
- Working on campus after the program end date.
